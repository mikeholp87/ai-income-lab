n8n vs Zapier vs Make: billing, builders and AI agents

n8n, Zapier and Make all connect apps and run steps for you. They differ in where they run, how they bill and how much control you get over each step.

This guide compares the three on the points that change your decision, with a link to each vendor's own page for every claim. Then it gives you a one-hour test so you can decide with your own workflow.

The short answer (October 2026)

  • Pick Zapier if you want the fastest setup and the widest app catalog, and you prefer a guided builder.
  • Pick Make if you want a visual canvas with routers and fine control over data, without hosting anything.
  • Pick n8n if you want to self-host, write code inside workflows when needed, or run long workflows without paying per step.

All three now offer AI agent features. That makes the agent feature a weak reason to choose one over the others.

Why the billing unit matters most

The billing unit decides what a long workflow costs you.

Take a workflow with ten steps that runs once.

  • On Zapier, each successful action step is a task, apart from the step types Zapier excludes, such as Filter, Paths and Formatter.
  • On Make, each module operation is at least one credit.
  • On n8n Cloud, it is one execution.

That does not make n8n cheaper for every case. Plan prices, limits and included volume differ, and they change. It does mean you should count steps and runs for your real workflow before you compare plan prices.

Builder style

Zapier

You add a trigger and then actions in order. It is the most guided of the three, which helps when you are new.

Make

You build on a canvas. Routers split the flow into branches, and filters decide which items pass between modules.

Run a one-hour test

Reading comparisons only goes so far. Build the same small workflow in each tool and decide from that.

Step 1: Pick one real workflow

Choose something you would actually use. A good test is a form submission that gets cleaned, sent to a sheet and announced in chat.

Step 2: Write the acceptance check first

Write down what a passing run looks like. For example, the email is lowercased, the row lands in the sheet and the chat message includes the name.

Step 3: Build it in each tool

Set a timer for 20 minutes per tool. Note where you got stuck.

Step 4: Run it five times

Send five test submissions, including one with a bad email. Record whether each run passed your check.

Step 5: Count the billing units

Open each tool's usage page and write down how many tasks, credits or executions the five runs used.

Step 6: Decide

You now have three facts per tool. Build time, pass rate on your check and units used. Pick the one that wins on the fact you care about most.

Common reasons people switch

These are patterns to watch for in your own use. They are not survey results.

  • Step count grows. Long workflows on per-step billing get expensive, which pushes people toward execution-based billing.
  • Data rules. Teams that must keep data on their own servers look at self-hosting.
  • Team skills. Teams without developers often stay on the more guided builders.

If self-hosting is the reason, also look at open-source options.

Frequently asked questions

How do n8n, Zapier, and Make count workflow usage?

n8n Cloud counts workflow executions, Zapier counts successful tasks, and Make counts credits for module actions. Read each vendor’s current billing rules for exceptions.

Which automation platform can I self-host?

n8n offers a self-hosted Community edition. Zapier and Make are hosted services in the comparison in this guide.

Which platform has the most app integrations?

Zapier lists 9,000+ apps and Make lists 3,500+ apps on their current vendor pages. n8n describes its integrations as available across plans and also supports custom nodes; the counting method is not directly comparable.

What is the entry paid plan price for each tool?

The at-a-glance table lists the starting paid plans checked on October 30, 2026. Prices, currencies, included usage, and billing terms can change, so confirm them on the linked vendor pricing pages.

How should I choose between the three tools?

Build the same small workflow with your own sample data, count its runs and steps, and compare the review, hosting, and data-location requirements against the vendor plan details.
