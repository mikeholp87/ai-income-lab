Quick answer

TubeAnalytics is my own YouTube analytics web app, a Node.js and Supabase app that reads YouTube Analytics and Data API data into dashboards, competitor comparison, idea and keyword tools. I built it with Claude Code, Gemini and OpenCode, about 150 hours over roughly two weeks by my count, and kept developing it afterward. The February demo showed several broken tabs, the March video toured the finished plans and tools, and the June session fixed a pricing-page 500 error and a dashboard redirect bug with an OpenCode debugging skill on a free model. Check the site for current plans.

This is my own product, so treat this as a build log, not an independent review. Figures such as the time it took and my domain rating are my own. I have a computer science degree but had not coded for a long time, so AI coding tools made a two-year project feel like two weeks. The OpenCode link on this site is an affiliate link. For the tools I used, see the OpenCode models and skills guide.

1. The February 2026 demo

The app ran in its own window while I built an audience and subscriber health feature in VS Code with OpenCode and Claude Opus 4.6. It uses Node.js and Supabase and reads the YouTube Analytics and Data API. The planned plans were Starter at 19 a month (15 billed annually) for one channel, Professional for three and Enterprise for five, with a seven-day free trial. The dashboard showed subscribers, views, engagement, retention and an optimization score. Several parts were unfinished: the behavior tab did not work, AI thumbnail testing was down because of a bug and I still wanted a contact form. I invited beta testers to join a waitlist.

2. The March 2026 tour

By March I described the product as built in about two weeks with Claude Code, Gemini 3.1 and OpenCode, with a change log of daily updates. Plans were Starter 19, Professional 49 and Enterprise 149 a month. The tour covered a dashboard of subscribers, views, watch hours and revenue, audience health, video retention and on-page SEO scores, a content calendar, competitor tracking with gap analysis, AI tools for ideas, thumbnail scoring and keywords, monetization scenarios, a comment manager and PDF reports. I had also sent a press release to build backlinks and quoted a domain rating of 39 from Ahrefs; that figure is mine and unaudited.

3. A June 2026 debugging day

The session started with a pricing page that threw a 500 error and a dashboard bug that sent logged-in users back to onboarding. In OpenCode on a free model I ran my systematic-debugging skill, which went through each problem. I then looked at the dashboard, whose revenue and performance data come from the YouTube API, and at Search Console and Vercel analytics for what to work on next. Starter allowed one channel and Enterprise up to five. My channel’s revenue over three months was almost 600 dollars on screen, which is my own figure.

4. What I would tell a builder

Prompt in small steps, keep a change log, and test every tab, because AI-built features often look finished while a tab is broken. Use a debugging skill instead of asking for a fix blindly, and keep billing, auth and redirects under tests. I did not show tests or a security review of the app in these videos.
