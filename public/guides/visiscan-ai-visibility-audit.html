VisiScan is Mike Holp's own product. Product site: https://www.visiscan.app. This page is the VisiScan scan and report walkthrough. It is not the Trakkr and Codex visibility workflow and not the Rank Prompt scan and MCP walkthrough.

The demo tests how a site shows up in AI answer engines. The four engines in this scan are ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Gemini. Scores, file checks and the $79 full-report fee below are what the recording showed on 22 July 2026.

What this recording shows

Mike opens VisiScan, runs a free site audit, and reads where the domain appears in those four answer engines. He then walks example reports, including a zero-visibility domain and a Hostinger scan, and finishes with a paid PDF generated from Codex. 1. Run a free domain scan

Enter a domain and run the scan. In the recording he types TubeAnalytics. He says that scan is free. The full report, on 22 July 2026, was a $79 one-time fee. Treat that price as what the recording showed that day, not as a later site price. He says the paid report is meant to list what you need to fix so the site can show up in ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Gemini.

While that paid run generates in Codex, he stays in the VisiScan UI and reads the live scan output. The recording does not speak a Codex prompt, so this page does not invent one.

2. Read readiness and visibility scores

For his own site the readiness score in this take is 56 out of 100. He says that needs work. Visibility is shown across the tested LLMs, with items that are already fine and items to fix. He describes the data on screen as accurate and up to date for that session.

One example domain, servemate.co.uk, shows 0% visibility, so it is not appearing in the answer engines this scan tests. Readiness on that report still looks relatively good. Suggested fixes on camera include schema, adding a telephone link, and Core Web Vitals, which he names as important for SEO and AEO. The local business JSON-LD on that site is flagged as not updated.

Hostinger is the other example. He calls it one of the largest hosting and domain providers, then says its visibility score still needs work. Those two reports are illustrations from 22 July 2026, not a ranking of those companies today.

3. How the audit checks LLMs and signals

He says you can copy findings into Codex or Claude Code and ask those tools to fix them, or hire someone to do that work. In his description, the agent reads the site, works out the market, then interrogates ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Gemini with real buyer questions localized to where the business operates.

The same overview says the audit covers 30-plus signals, including entity presence in Wikidata, Google Knowledge Graph, branded mentions, third-party citations, schema markup, AI crawler access, content and technical health. An agent-readiness ladder is on screen as well. On the Hostinger report he points at Core Web Vitals, branded web mentions and schema as items still to fix. For a self-hosted SEO stack with a separate data bill, see OpenSEO review: setup, data costs and practical limits.

4. Check sitemap, robots.txt and llms.txt

The scan looks for machine-readable files. In the recording he says LMS.text and AI.text. The chapters on the video label that section as llms.txt and AI crawler access. On the example in front of him, crawlers are allowed, which he treats as a good sign. He also shows an agent-readiness ladder and Core Web Vitals on Hostinger.

The audit finds Hostinger's sitemap and opens it. It also pulls his own sitemap. Schema data is listed as available. robots.txt is found for his site and, in this take, gives access to a lot of the AI crawlers. He wants those files accurate and up to date. The Tube Analytics scan also finds machine-readable pricing on the pricing page, and HTTPS enabled.

5. Review the recorded full report

He says you can paste the technical findings into Codex and ask it to fix them, then rerun the report to see whether the rating goes up. That later rerun is not in this video. Near the end, Codex finishes a full-tier run: 12 questions, 30 signal results and no worker errors, then one error is added and a PDF is generated. That job had been running about 17 minutes when he checked it.

The paid full report is what you got for $79 on 22 July 2026 in this recording. He opens the PDF on camera and says he is happy with what he has built. The video does not measure a later visibility change after fixes. A different build, tracking AI visibility as a SaaS, is AI Visibility Tracker SaaS build.
