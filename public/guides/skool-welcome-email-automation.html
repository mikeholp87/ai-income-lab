Skool Welcome Emails With Zapier, Make.com and EmailOctopus

Quick answer

Skool had no public API in July 2025, so use its Zapier integration to append each new member, with their answers and email, to a Google Sheet. In Make.com watch that sheet for new rows and add each person to EmailOctopus with first name and email, which starts a welcome sequence: an intro email, a 12-hour wait, a second email, a two-day wait and a last email. The catch: once my community became paid, I could no longer ask membership questions, so I could not collect emails from new members that way.

This is a July 2025 build for my own Skool community. It collects personal data, so only email people who joined and agreed to be contacted, and keep the sheet private. The Skool and Make.com links on this page are affiliate links. For plan details, see the Skool Hobby plan review.

1. Capture members in a sheet

On a free community you can turn on membership questions under plugins and ask for an email. Skool’s built-in Zapier integration then adds each approved member to a Google Sheet: first name, last name, the answers to your questions and the email. In Zapier you connect the Skool account and choose the sheet.

I looked at Apify actors that scrape Skool member data, then skipped them because they may break Skool’s terms of service. Use only the integration Skool provides.

2. Watch the sheet in Make.com

The Make.com scenario watches the sheet for new rows and adds each person to an EmailOctopus list with their email and first name. An optional WhatsApp Business Cloud module can message them as well, but I did not use it because I do not collect phone numbers. Setting it up goes through Meta for Developers.

3. The welcome sequence

In EmailOctopus the automation sends an introductory email, waits 12 hours, sends another, waits two days and sends the last. I like EmailOctopus: the recording said it has a free plan for small lists and is cheaper than Mailchimp as lists grow, but check its current pricing.

4. After the community went paid

When I made the community paid, membership questions stopped working, so I switched the Zap to the new paid member trigger. It writes first name, last name and email into a new sheet, and Make.com watches that sheet instead. People can also be invited to Skool through a webhook or a CSV import, in the same way.

What this recording did not cover

  • Skool’s current API and Zapier options, which may have changed since July 2025.
  • Open or click rates of the sequence.
  • Consent wording, which you need to add for your own audience and country.
