HeyGen AI avatar tutorial: create your first video

Quick answer

HeyGen turns a written script into a video of an AI avatar presenter speaking it. For your first video, open AI Studio, choose a stock avatar, paste a short script, check the voice, then generate a draft and review it sentence by sentence. On October 23, 2026 the Free plan listed 3 watermarked videos a month of up to 1 minute each.

The finish line is one useful video: a welcome message, a short explanation, or an introduction to a lesson. Keep the first attempt small enough to review sentence by sentence; a short clip is a practical way to learn how to use HeyGen. This HeyGen tutorial gives you a repeatable review before you automate. HeyGen AI avatar tutorial: set up your first video

Choose one audience and one action before opening the editor. For example: “Welcome new course members and ask them to complete the first exercise.” Write down where the video will appear so you can choose a sensible frame shape. Avoid starting with a complete course or a batch of social posts.

In HeyGen, open Create → Create a Video → Create in AI Studio. Choose landscape or portrait and start with a simple scene. The editor provides avatar, script, media, and text controls; its official walkthrough explains these panels. Keep the background plain for this first exercise. Source: AI Studio overview.

Choose a stock or custom HeyGen avatar

A stock avatar is a ready-made presenter from HeyGen’s public collection. A custom video avatar, called a Digital Twin in the help center, uses footage of a real person. The AI Studio overview describes the public avatar picker; the Create your first Digital Twin guide explains the personal footage route.

Start with stock when you mainly need to test whether the message is clear. Choose a custom avatar when viewers need to recognize you, such as a course introduction. For a useful comparison, keep the same script and assess whether the presenter fits the audience. Changing the script, voice, and presenter together makes it harder to identify what improved.

For your own avatar, open the Avatars area, select New Avatar, and choose Clone a Real Person. HeyGen documents recording live or importing footage. Use a quiet room, even lighting, and a clear view of your face; include your speaking audio. Follow the recording instructions for the creation flow you select. Source: Create your first Digital Twin.

Record consent and resolve verification issues

For a video-based Digital Twin, HeyGen requires a separate consent recording after the avatar footage. Read the exact text displayed, including any verification code. The same person must appear in both recordings. If you are preparing an avatar for somebody else, that person must record their own consent. Source: Recording your consent video.

Do this with the actual person on camera, with audible speech and a visible face. HeyGen rejects screen recordings of consent playback, and its troubleshooting page identifies identity mismatch, missing audio, and unclear codes as possible failures. Read the reason shown on the avatar card before trying again. Sources: Recording your consent video and Digital Twin creation error codes.

If verification fails, fix the named problem first. Check that the microphone recorded sound, the face remains visible, and the code was read clearly. Keep the original footage available while resolving the issue. Do not assume that uploading a file means the avatar has passed verification.

Write a spoken script and choose the voice

A first script needs an opening, one useful point, and a next step. Here is an example to adapt, rather than a claim about a particular course:

Welcome to the workshop. Today, choose one repetitive task and write down its input and expected result. Start with an example you can check by hand. When you finish, save your notes so you can compare them with your first automated run.

Read your draft aloud. Replace long sentences, spell out ambiguous abbreviations, and remove instructions such as “show the dashboard” from the spoken text. Put visual directions in your own production notes. A shorter script is easier to diagnose when pronunciation or pacing needs work.

In AI Studio, select a voice in the Voice panel and use Audio Preview to listen. HeyGen documents voice selection, speed and volume controls, and applying a voice across scenes. Check names, numbers, pauses, and the final sentence. Audio Preview does not show lip sync, expressions, or avatar animation; those require rendering. Source: Using voices in AI Studio.

Generate a draft, then review the whole result

Settle the narration before generating the final video. HeyGen recommends checking speech, previewing scenes, and reviewing motion and timing first. Changes that require an avatar scene to be rendered again consume credits. Source: Avatar and voice troubleshooting.

Generate the draft using the editor’s generation control, then review the finished output from beginning to end. Check whether the spoken words match the script, the mouth movement looks acceptable, and the framing leaves room for any text. If you add captions, check spelling and readability too. A good opening is not enough if the final instruction is clipped or unclear.

Keep a short revision list and change one problem at a time. Save the approved script beside the finished file. Your first success is a video you would willingly show its intended audience, with an accurate message and a clear next step.

If the clip goes on YouTube, the real test starts after it is published. Compare its retention and click-through rate with your other uploads before you make the next one. I have used TubeAnalytics to track YouTube retention and growth for that comparison; it is temporarily unavailable.

Automate with the API and Make.com after one good clip

The related videos are HeyGen avatar course: creation and automation limits and How to Scale Content with HeyGen + Make.com Automation. Use them as workflow demonstrations; verify present-day access and billing before copying settings.

HeyGen’s API uses separate pay-as-you-go credits, available independently of a paid web subscription. A successful manual video therefore doesn't prove that your automation has funding or access. Source: HeyGen API pricing explained. The current developer reference is reached through docs.heygen.com, which now redirects to developers.heygen.com.

At a high level, design the workflow around these checkpoints:

  1. Start with one approved record containing the final script and intended presenter.
  2. Submit a generation request with the correct avatar and voice identifiers. The Digital Twin API documentation shows these fields.
  3. Save the returned video identifier with the source record.
  4. Check status until completion or failure. HeyGen documents asynchronous generation and a returned video URL when complete. Source: Digital Twin API documentation.
  5. Download the result, review it, and mark the record complete only after that review.

Keep a failure branch that records the error and stops publishing. A fixed delay is not evidence that a render finished. The HeyGen API quick start explains status checks and failure messages. For the surrounding record mapping and approval practice, work through Build your first Make.com automation before expanding to a batch.

Frequently asked questions

Should I start with a stock avatar or my own avatar?

Start with a stock avatar if the immediate goal is to practice the script and review process. Choose your own avatar when your identity matters to the finished video.

Do I need a consent video for my Digital Twin?

Yes. HeyGen requires a consent recording for a video-based Digital Twin. The person in the avatar footage must also appear in the consent video and read the supplied text.

Source: Recording your consent video.

Does the voice preview show the finished avatar animation?

No. Audio Preview lets you check the narration. Lip sync, expressions, and avatar movement require a render.

Source: Using voices in AI Studio.

Can I use my web plan credits in Make.com?

No. HeyGen says Make integrations require an API key and separate API credits. Check API billing before running an automation.

Source: How to use credits on HeyGen.

What should I check before publishing?

Watch the full video with sound. Check pronunciation, factual claims, lip sync, framing, captions, and the final instruction. Keep the result as a draft until those checks pass.
