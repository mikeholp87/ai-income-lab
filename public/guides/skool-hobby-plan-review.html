Quick answer

In July 2025 Skool's Hobby plan was $9 a month with one admin and a 10% transaction fee, and no custom URL, plugins such as Zapier, sidebar links, Facebook pixel or hiding suggested communities. Pro was $99 a month with unlimited admins, a 2.9% fee and those extras. I downgraded two communities to Hobby, paying $18 a month for a free and a paid community, and kept my existing URLs.

This follows a July 2025 walkthrough made about a week after Skool launched the Hobby plan. Plan details may have changed since, so confirm them on Skool. The Skool link is an affiliate link and I earn recurring commission on paid sign-ups. For the later tiers feature, see the Skool membership tiers guide.

Free, premium and VIP pricing: membership tiers. Gating courses in the classroom: classroom upsell. Emailing new members automatically: welcome emails.

Who this is for and what you need

This guide is for a solo community owner deciding whether the $9 Hobby plan is enough or whether the $99 Pro plan is worth ten times the price. It compares the two plans as they stood in July 2025.

  • You are a good fit for Hobby if you are the only admin, you are happy with the Skool URL you were given, and you are not running paid ads to your About page yet.
  • You probably need Pro if you want Zapier or other plugins, sidebar links, the Facebook pixel for ad tracking, more than one admin, or the lower 2.9% fee on a busy paid community.
  • What you need: a Skool account, a card for the 14-day trial and a clear idea of whether your community will be free, paid or both.

1. Hobby and Pro side by side

Pro was $99 a month with unlimited members, courses and admins, a 2.9% transaction fee, a custom URL and hiding suggested communities. I compared fees: Discord 16%, Patreon 14%, another platform 13% and Stan 6%, against Skool Pro at 2.9% and Hobby at 10%. Hobby allowed one admin and had no custom URL or hiding of suggested communities, and, as I described them, no plugins such as Zapier, sidebar links or Facebook pixel.

2. Why I downgraded

I was on Pro for about a month, then moved both communities to Hobby: one free, one paid at $69 a month. I am the only admin and did not need the custom URL, so I pay $18 a month in total. Note the higher fee on the paid one: 10% of each payment.

3. Trial and affiliate link

I first said the trial was seven days and then corrected it: it is 14 days. Skool also has an affiliate program that pays recurring commission for as long as your referral stays a paying customer. My sign-up link is one.

4. About page, email and Discovery

Design the About page and classroom with a cover image, video and low-ticket courses. Membership questions now work on free and paid communities, so you can capture emails: I copy each new request into a spreadsheet and an EmailOctopus welcome sequence, then approve and message them. For ranking in Discovery I aimed for 100 to 200 activities a day, though my own days ranged from 1 to 78.

What changes day to day on Hobby

The plan difference shows up in a few concrete places. These are the ones I ran into after downgrading both of my communities.

No Zapier means manual member capture

Plugins and affiliates sat on the Pro side, so the Skool Zapier integration was not available on Hobby. When a new member request came in, I opened the pending request, copied the answer to my email question into a spreadsheet by hand and then approved them. If you want that step automated, the Skool welcome email build shows the Zapier and Make.com version, which needs a plan with plugins.

No Facebook pixel means no ad conversion tracking

The Facebook pixel tracks the join button on your About page. Without it you can still run ads, but you cannot see which clicks became members. I had run Facebook ads aimed at the Philippines, where clicks were cheap, then paused them and relied on Skool Discovery instead.

The About page matters more than the plan

My About page used a designed cover image with the group title, a short Loom video showing what members get and an image of the classroom. Downgrading reset my conversion counter, and right after it showed 95 About page visitors and 66 sign-ups, about 69%. The classroom held low-ticket items, a $19 course and $39 automation templates, so a free community could still sell. The Skool community setup guide walks through building that page.

Discovery works on both plans

A Hobby community still appears in Discover communities. Mine sat around page seven or eight of the tech category, at number 196 on the day I recorded. Skool ranks on activity, so I aimed for 100 to 200 activities a day. For a wider look at selling knowledge online, see AI side hustles for beginners.

FAQ

What did Hobby cost?

$9 a month in July 2025, with a 10% transaction fee.

What do you lose on Hobby?

extra admins, a custom URL, plugins such as Zapier, sidebar links, the Facebook pixel and hiding suggested communities.

How long is the free trial?

14 days, after I corrected my first statement of seven.

Can I use Zapier on the Hobby plan?

Not in July 2025. Plugins and affiliates were Pro only, so on Hobby I added new members to my spreadsheet by hand.

Do I need Pro to run Facebook ads to my community?

You can run ads on Hobby, but the Facebook pixel that tracks the join button was Pro only, so you cannot measure conversions without it.
